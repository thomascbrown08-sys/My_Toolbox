A short, honest introduction to the patterns behind common struggles, and the treatments that actually address them — written so you can bring an informed question to a session, not so you can diagnose yourself before one.

Two kinds of entries live here. Patterns name a mechanism — what's actually happening, and why it happens. Modalities describe a treatment — what it looks like in practice, and what it's for. Every pattern points toward the modalities that address it. Nothing here replaces a clinician; it's meant to make the first conversation with one more useful.

Patterns — naming the mechanism

  • The double bind

    When every available response gets punished. Read this if any choice you make in a relationship feels like it's already wrong.

  • Disorganized attachment

    When the person you needed safety from was also the danger.

  • Learned helplessness

    Why acting on your own behalf can still feel pointless or dangerous, long after it stopped being either.

  • The complex-trauma triad

    Dysregulated emotion, a self-concept that assumes the worst, and difficulty with closeness — why these three travel together.

  • Moral injury

    The wound of having actually done wrong — distinct from fear-based trauma, and treated differently.

  • Parentification

    When a child is made responsible for an adult's needs, instead of the other way around.

  • Toxic shame vs. guilt

    "I did something bad" and "I am bad" aren't the same sentence, and they don't resolve the same way.

  • Godly grief vs. worldly grief

    Paul's own distinction — sorted by what the sorrow produces, not by how heavy it feels.

  • Emotional flashbacks

    A flood of an old feeling with no memory attached — easy to mistake for overreacting to the present.

  • Repetition compulsion

    Why familiar can feel safer than good, even when it isn't good at all.

  • Splitting

    Sorting people, or yourself, into wholly good or wholly bad, with no working middle.

  • Codependency

    When your own stability fuses with managing someone else's — and real love becomes the lock.

Modalities — what treatment involves

  • Cognitive behavioral therapy

    Structured, skills-based, and usually the first thing offered. What a session actually involves.

  • EMDR

    A specific protocol for processing a specific memory — not talk therapy, and not for everything.

  • ACT

    Values as an anchor, not a mood to chase.

  • Somatic / polyvagal-informed work

    Working with the body, not just the story about it.

  • Adaptive Disclosure

    The named treatment for moral injury specifically.

  • Confession & spiritual direction

    A distinctly Christian modality, not a coat of paint on a secular one.

  • Family systems therapy

    Seeing a pattern across generations, not just in one person.

  • Sabbath as regulation

    Rest that isn't earned — a weekly argument against a trained belief that it has to be.

  • Christian recovery groups

    A synthesis of accountability and gospel content that's already been built.

  • Lament as practice

    A structured way to tell God the truth about pain.

  • Puritan self-examination

    A daily reckoning with the heart — including a real warning against doing it too much.
