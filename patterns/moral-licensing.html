Pattern

Moral licensing

How a good deed quietly buys permission for a poor one.

Definition

A cognitive bias in which having done something good gives a person unconscious permission to do something less good afterward — the mental ledger balances itself before the second act even happens. The counterweight to moral injury: where moral injury is the wound of having genuinely done wrong, moral licensing is the mechanism that lets wrong happen more easily, quietly justified by a prior credit.

The mechanism

A good act is banked as credit

The mind registers the good deed as a kind of moral surplus, available for later use.

The surplus authorizes a withdrawal

Permission for the next, lesser act gets quietly drawn from that same account.

The ledger runs beneath conscious awareness

Rarely a deliberate calculation — usually just a felt sense of having earned some slack.

What it produces over time

A pattern of behavior that can look inconsistent from outside — real generosity paired with real lapses — which makes more sense once the underlying ledger logic is visible, rather than being read as simple hypocrisy.

Where this shows up

Anyone whose self-account runs on comparison and balance rather than a settled identity — which is a pattern this site has already named a different, more permanent answer for.

Look up

Grace was never a ledger system, which is exactly why it can't be gamed by one. A good deed was never meant to purchase permission for the next wrong — that only makes sense if righteousness is something to accumulate, rather than something already, fully given.
