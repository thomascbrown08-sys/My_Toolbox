Pattern

Over-responsibility for others' feelings

If you are upset, I did it, and I can fix it.

Definition

The felt conviction that other people's emotional states are yours to manage or repair. Narrower and more ordinary than codependency, which fuses identity and stability to one specific relationship — this pattern shows up even in people not fused with anybody in particular, simply exhausted by every room they enter.

The mechanism

Someone else's feeling registers as my task

Not a concern to notice — a job to complete.

Repair feels mandatory, not optional

Leaving someone else's feeling to belong to them isn't experienced as an available option.

The cost accumulates invisibly

The labor produces nothing to point to afterward, so its price goes uncounted — including by the person paying it.

What it produces over time

A life quietly organized around forecasting and managing other people's moods, chronic depletion with no obvious cause, and real difficulty telling genuine care apart from compulsive management — they can feel identical from the inside.

Where this shows up

Any relational environment where one person's calm was treated as someone else's job — a household, a workplace, a friendship carried unevenly for years.

Related tools

  • CodependencyThe fused-identity version of this same pull
  • Family systems therapyFor seeing where the role was assigned
  • Confession & spiritual directionA place to set the task down honestly

Look up

If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all (Romans 12:18). The limit is built into the command itself — twice. Years spent failing at total responsibility for every room's peace were years spent failing at something that was never actually asked.
