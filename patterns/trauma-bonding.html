Pattern

Trauma bonding

The bond itself, named on its own — not a reflection of judgment, but a predictable outcome of the mechanism that produced it.

Definition

Where intermittent reinforcement names the mechanism, this page names what it produces: a strong, often confusing attachment to someone who is a source of both fear and comfort. The term comes from work on exploitative relationships and describes something specific — not ordinary love, and not simple codependency, but a bond whose strength is traceable to how unpredictably harm and warmth were distributed by the same person.

The mechanism

The bond is an output, not a choice

Its strength is a predictable result of the reinforcement pattern that formed it — not a reflection of the bonded person's judgment, worth, or intelligence.

Separation can produce real withdrawal

Because the intermittent good moments functioned like a genuine reward, losing access to them can produce a response that resembles withdrawal from any strong reinforcer — not weakness, a documented pattern.

Knowing it's harmful doesn't loosen it

Clearly understanding that a relationship is dangerous and still feeling pulled toward it aren't contradictory under this mechanism. They're accurate readings of two different things — the relationship's danger, and the bond's independent strength.

What it produces over time

Repeated cycles of leaving and returning, each one often accompanied by fresh shame about the return — shame that misreads the bond's strength as a personal failing rather than what it actually is: a specific, well-documented consequence of how the relationship was structured. The bond having formed this strongly is not evidence against the person it formed in.

Where this shows up

Overlaps heavily with disorganized attachment and coercive control, but is worth naming as its own thing — recognizing this is a documented bond, not a personal weakness is frequently part of what starts to loosen its grip, independent of any other work being done.

A trauma bond is exactly that kind of yoke — genuinely hard to shed, and still not a permanent condition. Standing firm is named here as an active thing, not a feeling to wait for.
