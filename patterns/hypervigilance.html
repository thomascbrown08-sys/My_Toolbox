Pattern

Hypervigilance

The scanning that once kept a child safe, and never fully learned to switch off.

Definition

A persistent, low-grade scanning of a room, a face, a tone — for danger that made sense to track closely once and now runs continuously, whether or not real risk is present. Distinct from general anxiety: hypervigilance is specifically threat-detection, and it's frequently accurate in what it notices, which is part of what makes it so hard to put down.

The mechanism

Threat-detection calibrated by real danger

The scanning was tuned somewhere it was genuinely needed.

The system doesn't know the danger ended

Calibration doesn't expire automatically just because circumstances changed.

Accuracy reinforces the habit

Because the reading is often correct, there's rarely clean evidence available to argue the scanning isn't necessary.

What it produces over time

Exhaustion from a mind that's never fully off duty, a reputation for being unusually perceptive that comes at a constant hidden cost, and real difficulty relaxing even in rooms that are genuinely safe.

Where this shows up

Rarely alone — it sits underneath several other patterns already on this site. Disorganized attachment, the complex-trauma triad, emotional flashbacks, and coercive control all reliably produce it.

Look up
