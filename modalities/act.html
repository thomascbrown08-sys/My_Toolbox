Acceptance and Commitment Therapy

Values as an anchor, not a mood to chase.

What it actually is

ACT starts from a different premise than most talk therapy: trying to eliminate a difficult thought or feeling often makes it louder, not quieter — the effort to not feel anxious becomes its own source of anxiety. So instead of fighting the thought, ACT works on changing your relationship to it, so it stops functioning as the thing steering your choices, while you keep acting according to what actually matters to you, discomfort and all.

What a session actually looks like

A good amount of early work is values clarification — not goals, but the deeper things a goal is usually in service of. Alongside that, ACT uses what's called defusion: learning to notice a thought as a thought passing through, rather than a fact you're required to obey. A therapist might have you say a distressing belief in a silly voice, or picture it written on a leaf floating down a stream — not to dismiss it, but to loosen its grip enough that it stops making the decision for you. Then the practical work: choosing to act on a value even while the uncomfortable thought or feeling is still present, rather than waiting for it to leave first.

What it's especially good for

Chronic avoidance, and situations where trying to get rid of a feeling has become its own trap — anxiety about anxiety, shame about still feeling ashamed. It's also useful wherever values have become foggy or borrowed rather than actually held, since so much of the method depends on knowing what you're actually anchoring to.

What it doesn't replace

ACT isn't primarily built to process a specific traumatic memory the way EMDR is, and it can be misapplied as a way to simply tolerate an ongoing harmful situation rather than change it. It's a way of acting well despite internal weather, not a substitute for addressing weather that shouldn't be there in the first place.

Look up

ACT asks what you're actually anchored to. For a Christian, that question has an answer that isn't self-generated the way a typical ACT values exercise assumes — your deepest commitments aren't preferences you constructed, they're obligations and loves you've received. That makes the anchor more stable, not less real: it doesn't evaporate on a bad day the way a self-authored value can, because it was never yours to author in the first place.
