Modality

Christian recovery groups

A synthesis of structured accountability and gospel content that's already been built — worth knowing it exists.

What it actually is

Twelve-step recovery's own roots run back through the Oxford Group's Christian revivalism, so this synthesis is less an import than a retrieval. Programs like Celebrate Recovery took the twelve-step structure — honest admission, accountability, sponsorship, regular meeting rhythm — and grounded each step explicitly in Scripture rather than a generic "higher power." The structural genius of the twelve-step model, regular confession in community with people who won't be shocked, stays intact; the content becomes explicitly the gospel rather than a spirituality left undefined.

What this actually looks like

Weekly meetings, usually with a large-group teaching time followed by small, gender-specific accountability groups organized around a shared "hurt, habit, or hangup" — not limited to substance addiction, though that's the most visible use case. A sponsor or accountability partner provides ongoing contact between meetings. The rhythm matters as much as the content: consistent, low-barrier, and structured so that showing up itself becomes a practiced discipline rather than something dependent on motivation that day.

What it's especially good for

Any pattern that thrives in secrecy and isolation — codependency, moral injury, addiction, chronic shame — since the structure itself is built to interrupt secrecy specifically, on a schedule, whether or not you feel ready that week.

What it doesn't replace

Peer accountability isn't clinical treatment, and a well-run group will say so directly — complex trauma, moral injury requiring specialized treatment, or a condition needing medication management still needs a trained clinician alongside the group, not instead of it.

  • Moral injuryStructured, ongoing confession in community

Look up

Confess your sins to one another was never meant to happen once, in private, and be finished. It was meant to be a practiced rhythm inside a real community — which is precisely what this modality restores as a structure, not just a suggestion.
