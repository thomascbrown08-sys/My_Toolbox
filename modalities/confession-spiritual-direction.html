Confession & spiritual direction

A distinctly Christian modality, not a coat of paint on a secular one.

What it actually is

Two related but distinct practices. Confession is naming sin honestly — to God directly, and, per James 5:16, to a trusted other as well, since shame tends to lose its grip in proportion to how honestly and safely it's spoken aloud. Spiritual direction is a longer-running practice: regular meetings with someone trained to help you notice where God is already at work in your life and attend to it, distinct from therapy's clinical frame and distinct from a single confession's momentary act.

What this actually looks like

Confession can be formal — to a pastor or elder, sometimes within a structured practice of the church — or informal, to a trusted brother or sister who will hold what's said with both honesty and grace. Spiritual direction usually runs monthly or so, unhurried, less about solving a problem in the session and more about noticing a pattern over time: where is resistance showing up, where is God's kindness actually landing, what does the next faithful step look like. Neither one is a performance. Both depend on being genuinely known by the other person.

What it's especially good for

Shame that thrives specifically in secrecy — moral injury and scrupulosity both tend to isolate the person carrying them, and isolation is usually what keeps the wound from closing. It also reaches the relational and spiritual dimension no clinical modality is built to address, by design: the actual restoration of communion with God and with a community, not only symptom relief.

What it doesn't replace

This isn't a substitute for treating a clinical-level condition — moral injury this deep, or complex trauma, usually needs a trained clinician alongside it, not instead of it. And it can be misused the way any good thing can: doctrine or ritual deployed to skip past feeling rather than to hold it. If confession starts functioning as a way to avoid affect rather than process it honestly, that's worth naming to whoever you're confessing to.
