Somatic / polyvagal-informed work

Working with the body, not just the story about it.

What it actually is

Built largely on polyvagal theory, which maps how the nervous system moves between states of safety, mobilized threat response, and shutdown. The core insight is that safety is first a physical, felt state, not a belief — you can know intellectually that you're safe and still have a body that hasn't caught up. Somatic approaches work with the body directly: breath, posture, movement, and close, gentle attention to physical sensation, rather than starting from the story about what happened.

What a session actually looks like

Slower than most people expect. A practitioner might ask you to notice where a feeling lives in the body, and simply stay with that sensation briefly rather than analyzing it — a small, deliberately titrated dose, not full immersion. The therapist's own calm, regulated presence does real work here too; a nervous system can borrow steadiness from another person's before it can generate its own. Over time, the aim is a wider capacity to stay present with sensation without being overwhelmed by it — sometimes called widening the window of tolerance.

What it's especially good for

Fawn and freeze patterns, chronic hypervigilance, and the common experience of having full cognitive insight into a pattern — you can explain it perfectly — while the body still reacts as though nothing has changed. When talking about a pattern hasn't translated into feeling safe, this is often why.

What it doesn't replace

It doesn't, by itself, provide the meaning-making or narrative integration that makes a hard history make sense as a whole story. It's usually most effective paired with talk-based or spiritually integrated work, not as a stand-alone replacement for either.

Look up

Scripture has never treated the body as an afterthought to be managed by the mind — you were made embodied, and the body you carry is part of what's being redeemed, not merely a vehicle for the soul to escape from. Working with the body isn't a concession to something less spiritual. It's taking seriously that God made it, and that it, too, needs to learn it's safe.
