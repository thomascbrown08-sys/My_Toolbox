Deprogramming

Replacing a definition, not processing a feeling — and not something completed alone.

What it actually is

A recovery approach distinct from standard emotional processing. Rather than working through feelings about a past experience, deprogramming targets an installed definition directly — replacing a false operating rule with a true one, through sustained exposure to real evidence against it, not through insight alone. Insight can recognize that a definition is false in a single moment. Replacing it takes considerably longer, and runs on a different mechanism entirely.

What this actually looks like

This isn't a single, manualized protocol the way EMDR or Adaptive Disclosure are — it's closer to a sustained orientation than a named technique. Practically, it means treating a recurring expectation as a testable claim rather than a mood to sit with — love costs something isn't a feeling to process, it's a specific proposition that can be tested against new, real evidence, gathered slowly, in a relationship where the opposite is actually demonstrated, repeatedly, over enough time to matter. A definition installed through lived experience is rarely replaced by an argument. It has to be replaced by a different lived experience, sustained long enough to genuinely compete with the old one.

What it's especially good for

A definition that has survived extensive feelings-focused work — someone who has done real grief work, real anger work, real lament, and still finds an old rule firing automatically in new situations. That combination is usually the sign that what's needed next is replacement, not more processing.

What it doesn't replace

It doesn't address the acute feelings attached to how a definition was originally installed — those still need their own processing first, through whatever that specific pattern calls for. And per its own logic, it doesn't work as a private, internal exercise: a true definition has to be encountered in an actual relationship that embodies it, not arrived at through reasoning alone.

Look up

Put off your old self... and put on the new self, created after the likeness of God (Ephesians 4:22–24). Put off, put on — not felt through. Scripture's own language for this work is already closer to replacement than to processing, which is worth noticing rather than assuming the two were always the same instruction.
