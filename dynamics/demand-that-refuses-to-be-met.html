The demand that refuses to be met

When an accusation survives its own remedy, the accusation was never about the remedy.

Definition

A demand is issued, framed as the one thing that would resolve a conflict or restore trust. The exact compliance demanded is offered — not partially, not defensively, precisely as specified. The offer is then refused, and converted into further evidence against the person who met it. If genuine compliance had actually been the goal, meeting it would have ended the matter. That it doesn't is the diagnostic sign this dynamic leaves behind.

Related to the double bind, but distinct from it. A double bind traps someone between two demands where no response is safe. This is narrower: a single demand, genuinely satisfied, whose satisfaction is then discarded rather than honored.

The mechanism

A specific demand is issued

Framed as the one condition standing between the current conflict and its resolution.

The exact demand is met

Compliance is offered precisely as specified — not a close approximation, the actual thing that was asked for.

The offer is refused and reused as evidence

Instead of resolving anything, the compliance itself becomes proof of some new, previously unstated failing — often some version of "if you'd really meant it, you wouldn't have needed to be asked."

What it produces over time

Years spent chasing a moving target, since every satisfied demand simply generates the next one — without it ever becoming obvious that satisfaction was never the mechanism's actual purpose. The demand's real function is to keep a person permanently in the position of trying to earn something that was never going to be given, regardless of what they did to earn it.

Where this shows up

Relationships and institutions where the stated grounds for approval or restored trust keep shifting the moment they're actually met — distinct from a standard that's legitimately still unmet, which stays satisfied once it genuinely has been.

Related tools

  • The double bindRelated but distinct — no safe response, versus a met demand discarded
  • DARVOA similar recognizable sequence, run when confronted directly
  • Coercive controlOften the wider apparatus this tactic operates inside

Look up

God is not man, that he should lie... Has he said, and will he not do it? (Numbers 23:19). A promise that moves the moment it's kept was never actually a promise. That's not how the real thing behaves, which is exactly how you can tell the difference.
