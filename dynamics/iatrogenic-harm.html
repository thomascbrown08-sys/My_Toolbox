Iatrogenic harm

Harm caused by the treatment itself — not the condition it was meant to address.

Definition

"Iatrogenic" literally means physician-caused. It names harm that comes from care itself: medication mismanaged, dosing changed too quickly, side effects minimized or dismissed, a patient's own reports filed under the diagnosis instead of investigated on their own merits. It's distinct from the underlying condition — this is damage the treatment relationship added on top of it.

The mechanism

Harm from the treatment, not just the condition

The care relationship itself becomes a source of injury, alongside or instead of providing relief.

Reports treated as symptoms rather than information

When what a patient says about their own experience gets absorbed into the diagnosis instead of taken as a data point worth checking.

Trust in care itself becomes the casualty

Unlike other harms, this one specifically damages a person's ability to seek help from the very systems built to provide it.

What it produces over time

Reluctance to report new symptoms, distrust of future providers even well-intentioned ones, and a learned expectation that raising a concern will be met with dismissal rather than investigation — which can lead people to stop reporting real problems at exactly the point they need to be heard most.

Where this shows up

Long-term psychiatric medication management, chronic illness treatment, and any extended care relationship where a patient's own account of what's happening to them was repeatedly discounted rather than examined.

Related tools

  • Testimonial injusticeThe same discounting mechanism, in a medical setting
  • Institutional betrayalWhen the whole system, not one provider, failed to respond

Look up

The Great Physician never treats a report of pain as merely a symptom to be managed away without first being heard. A system that dismissed yours was not modeling how you were actually attended to the whole time.
