What a diagnosis means

Read this before you look anything else up here.

A diagnosis is a description, not a verdict. It's a clinician's way of saying: enough of your experience matches a known pattern that the treatments known to help that pattern are worth trying on you too. That's genuinely useful information. It is not a statement about your worth, your permanence, or the whole shape of who you are.

What a label actually delineates

Every diagnosis draws a line through a spectrum, not a fence around a category you either belong to or don't. Most people who read a description of anxiety, complex trauma, or a mood disorder will recognize something of themselves in it — because these labels describe common human responses to difficulty, stretched to a degree that starts interfering with life. Recognizing a piece of yourself in a description is not the same as meeting the threshold the label requires. That threshold — how much, how often, how disruptive — is exactly the judgment a trained clinician is there to make. This page will tell you what a pattern is and what it tends to respond to. It will not tell you whether you have it.

The trap worth naming directly

It's possible to read pages like these looking for relief and end up more anxious than before — checking symptom lists, hunting for the label that finally explains everything, treating the search itself as the work. If that starts happening, the search has become the symptom, not the solution. The purpose of every page here is to help you ask a clinician a sharper question, not to let you arrive at your own conclusion and stop there.

How to actually use these pages

Read a pattern page. If it sounds like you, bring it to whoever you're already seeing — a therapist, a doctor, a pastor — and ask them directly whether it fits, and if so, which of the linked treatments they'd recommend and why. That's the whole use case. These pages are built to make that one conversation go better, not to replace it.

Look up

A diagnosis, rightly used, is a mercy — it says you are not simply and mysteriously broken, that there is a mechanism, and mechanisms can be worked with. But no label was ever meant to be where you live. Your standing before God does not wait on a clinician's judgment, and it was never going to be settled by one. Whatever this page helps you name, the naming is in service of trusting Him with it — actively, not just believing something true about Him from a safe distance, but bringing the actual pattern to the actual God who is not surprised by it and has not moved.

My Toolbox — a companion resource, not a substitute for a clinician or a pastor.
